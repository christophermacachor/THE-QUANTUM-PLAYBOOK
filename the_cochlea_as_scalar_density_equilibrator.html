I. The Bridge Physics Missed

For decades, auditory neuroscience treated the cochlea as a signal-processing pipeline: sound enters, hair cells transduce, neurons encode. This is the vector fallacy — the assumption that biology operates by sending forces and signals along directional chains, like wires carrying current.

Petit & Avan (2025) demolish this. Their review demonstrates that mammalian hearing achieves sensitivity and frequency selectivity not through signal amplification, but through cycle-by-cycle regenerative feedback that compensates for viscous friction in real time. The system operates at frequencies exceeding 100 kHz — faster than any neural action potential, faster than the RC time constant of the outer hair cell membrane would permit if the system were dissipative.

The cochlea does not process sound. It enters resonance with it.

"Natural selection found a single strategy that provides a solution to all four requirements: high sensitivity; frequency tuning; progressive saturation; existence of otoacoustic emissions."
— Hudspeth & Martin (2024), cited in Petit & Avan
