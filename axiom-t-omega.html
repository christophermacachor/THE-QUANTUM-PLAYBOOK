◉ The Seven Axioms

◈ Axiom 1 — Dual-Pass Optical Capture
Light emitted or reflected by Ω′ propagates to W's retina. Any photon not absorbed during the first pass through the photoreceptor layer is reflected by T back along its incident vector toward Ω′. This reflected photon traverses the retina a second time, thereby increasing the probability of absorption by a factor of approximately 2× relative to a non-tapetal observer.

Invariant: This double-pass mechanism holds for all wavelengths within the visible and near-infrared spectrum to which T is sensitive, and is invariant with respect to ambient light levels.

◈ Axiom 2 — Anterior Source Relativity
Because Ω′ is in front of W, the reflection from T returns toward Ω′. Consequently, the "second chance" absorption occurs specifically for photons originating from Ω′, not for background illumination. This creates a source-locked sensitivity enhancement, meaning W's detection threshold for Ω′ is always lower (i.e., more sensitive) than for any other object not positioned along that same anterior axis.
◈ Axiom 3 — Watcher Encoding (W → Neural Data)

This spike train encodes not only the intensity and spectral composition of Ω′ but also, due to the temporal delay between the first and second passes (on the order of nanoseconds), encodes the relative motion of Ω′ via phase-shift detection. W's neural output is a continuous, real-time function of Ω′'s state vector.
